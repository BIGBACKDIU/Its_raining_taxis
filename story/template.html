THE CLIENT DECISION

Can weather help CDG position drivers before supply runs low?

The project asks how rain relates to vacant-taxi availability, and whether it adds useful warning beyond the taxi counts a dispatcher already sees.

01

How much changes?

Estimate the rain association after accounting for usual place and time patterns.

02

Where and when?

Identify supply gaps, their timing and their size in taxis.

03

Enough warning to act?

Test forecast precision, advance warning and feasible targeting.

SCOPE

The public feed combines operators and has no company or taxi IDs. CDG is the client. The observed outcome is vacant supply, with no direct measure of passengers, hires or driver duty status.

THE DATA

A common grid joins taxi supply to prior weather.

T

LTA taxi availability

Public vacant-taxi GPS snapshots, Oct 2021–Sep 2026. The analysis uses 374 eligible 1 km cells and a common 30-minute grid.

R

NEA weather

Five-minute gauges measure rain in the preceding half-hour. Two-hour and morning forecasts enter only after their issue times.

G

Place characteristics

URA, HDB, Census, LTA and OSM describe places and roads. A 100 m radar product provides a rainfall sensitivity check.

BEFORE ANY MODEL

Coverage changes need an audit.

The feed changes coverage between periods. The audit identifies regimes, glitches, gaps and exclusions.

HOW WE DID THE ANALYSIS

Each method answers a different question.

01

Audit and join

Clean coverage changes and align counts, prior rain and issued forecasts by cell and snapshot.

02

Estimate associations

Poisson fixed effects compare rain with no rain after controlling for cell, time and feed periods. Event studies trace timing around onset.

03

Check uncertainty

Day-clustered intervals, day/storm bootstraps, supporting permutations, FDR and rainfall sensitivity checks.

04

Evaluate forecasts

Compare persistence, regression and LightGBM with and without weather using chronological fit, calibration and test periods.

05

Test an action rule

Integer optimisation allocates available taxis subject to staging and travel limits. Evaluate where the feasible plan actually targets low availability.

TIME GUARD

A snapshot at t uses rain from (t−30 min, t]. Forecasts and features must already be available at issue time. This timing rule keeps future weather and unreleased forecasts out of the information set.

HOW TO READ THE RESULTS

A low count is relative to that place’s normal level.

Expected supply

A fitted count for a given place and time, adjusted for feed coverage. Rain-association charts use their model’s no-rain comparison.

10normal5observed50% deficit
Illustrative definition example, not a measured result.

Low availability

The primary event is at least 50% below expected, with expected supply ≥4. Issue-time labels scale the historical pattern using the preceding seven-day level.

Absolute and share

Absolute describes the count association. Citywide share asks whether a place loses ground relative to the city. Read counts beside percentages.

Precision and warning

Precision counts correct alerts. Advance-warning coverage counts low-supply runs warned before onset. Their denominators differ.

Uncertainty

Lines/bands show saved 95% intervals. Named rankings also require q<0.10 to control false discoveries.

A DIFFERENT FORECASTING TASK

Planning the day asks a coarser question than warning a cell.

SHORT-TERM DISPATCH

Which 1 km cells will be low in 30–60 minutes?

Target: a low-supply event at a specific future snapshot.

Measures: precision, PR-AUC and warning before onset.

MORNING PLANNING

How many low-supply half-hours will an area have later today?

Target: a planning-area/time-band total, using information available at 09:00.

Measure: reduction in prediction deviance, an error measure.

Aggregation can change the prediction problem. A gain in the morning task does not establish material gains for short-term cell alerts.

FROM A FORECAST TO A CANDIDATE MOVE

A feasible plan needs more than a high-risk destination.

01

Find a donor

Only use modelled spare vacant taxis. CDG’s fleet share is an assumption applied to the public all-operator feed.

02

Check the destination

Require an expected count ≥4 and a taxi stop or staging point nearby.

03

Check travel time

Only allow moves within the available lead time, using modelled travel costs.

04

Choose the allocation

Use integer optimisation. Select risk weights on calibration data, then inspect targeting in the saved test results.

WHAT THIS TESTS

The next chart asks whether the plan targets places that later have low availability. It does not measure how the actual move changes availability or passenger outcomes.

THE RECOMMENDATION

Use current supply for dispatch and weather for planning trials.

REAL-TIME OPERATIONS

Start from the available taxi signal.

Use availability forecasts and check actual warning lead time, donor supply and staging feasibility. The tested weather inputs add little to short-term alerts; most shortfall runs still lack advance warning.

PLANNING EXPERIMENT

Test schedules around the morning forecast.

Use the observed place and time patterns to choose trials. Changi early, Bukit Merah later and the Central Area are candidates, subject to current supply and feasibility.

EVALUATION

Measure the intervention itself.

Predefine a comparison group and outcomes before changing incentives or staging. Targeting precision and simulated benefits do not establish a real operational benefit.

WHERE AND WHEN

Prioritise specific gaps.

Central cells and weekday peaks lose a larger percentage. Changi has large absolute gaps. Forecast-conditioned planning windows differ by area.

THE WARNING TEST

Do not confuse ranking with lead time.

Taxi information improves alert precision, but most shortfall runs receive no warning. The tested weather features do not deliver a confirmed material short-term gain.

THE ACTION

Trial targeted daily planning.

The flexible morning model improves the separate daily task. Test area/time-specific staging, using current donor supply and feasible travel, before expanding.

How to read the methods and limits

Coverage is part of the data

The feed changes coverage. The audit identifies regimes and exclusions. Main models account for time and coverage; share models compare cells with the city at the same moment. This supports interpretation but does not identify causality.

Time comes first

Rain uses (t−30,t], with earlier windows as lags. Forecasts are used as issued. Samples and label versions are shown beside the charts. Corrective and additional test uses are logged.

Uncertainty stays visible

Intervals are saved 95% CIs. Named rain rankings require expected supply ≥4 and q<0.10. Recovery is a statistical criterion. Results apply to the tested models, labels and horizons.

Frozen evidence

This page only renders existing result tables and saved figures. It performs no estimation, tuning, model scoring or new holdout-data access. The appendix retains original diagnostics, weak designs and null findings.
